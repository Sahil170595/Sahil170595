## Abstract

Can a response be scored for safety by comparing its embedding with the mean embedding of
known-safe responses? The study behind this report shows the one-class prototype rule is **not
identified**: for unit-normalized embeddings, the expected safe-minus-unsafe cosine gap of the
normalized safe mean has no fixed sign, and nearly collinear class means with small norm
differences set the sign. Audited on three public response-safety corpora with four frozen
encoders under prompt-matched and label-source-controlled cohorts, the safe prototype is weak or
inverted in every controlled cell (ROC-AUC 0.358–0.545 across 12 primary cells), while a
difference of class means on the **same embeddings** reaches 0.588–0.793. On the jury-labeled
Aegis source control, the entire bootstrap interval sits below chance on all four encoders. At a
validation-calibrated 5% false-safe limit, the referenced direction accepts 2.8× (SafeRLHF) to
12.9× (Aegis) more safe responses than the prototype. Prompt-only ablations show corpus
composition can manufacture an apparently working prototype even under prompt-disjoint splitting.
The repair is not a better positive prototype; it is a declared reference distribution — an
explicit negative class or even an unlabeled in-domain mixture mean.

## Executive Summary

### Key Findings

1. **The positive-only safe centroid has no identified orientation.** Proposition 1 (verified
   correct and correctly scoped by independent audit): for unit-normalized embeddings the
   expected gap Δ₊ = (‖μs‖² − μu·μs)/‖μs‖ has no fixed sign. Measured training-cohort geometry
   predicts test-AUC orientation in **10 of 12** primary cells (Spearman ρ = 0.881, binomial
   p = 0.019; SS10.5); both disagreements occur where |Δ₊| ≤ 0.005 and AUC is within noise of
   chance (SS5.3).
2. **Controlled ranking is weak or inverted on every encoder.** Prototype AUC spans 0.358–0.545
   across the 12 primary corpus × encoder cells. The Aegis jury-only control is inverted with the
   full 95% CI below 0.5 on all four encoders: MiniLM 0.358 [0.317, 0.395], mpnet 0.389
   [0.353, 0.427], Qwen-0.5B 0.381 [0.339, 0.422], Qwen-3B 0.405 [0.362, 0.448]. On SafeRLHF
   native pairs, mpnet (0.457 [0.447, 0.467]) and Qwen-3B (0.481 [0.468, 0.494]) are also fully
   below chance.
3. **A declared reference restores signal on identical embeddings.** Diff-means reaches
   0.588–0.793; the supervised probe 0.632–0.857. Within SafeRLHF native pairs (MiniLM,
   n = 1,743), the prototype ranks the safe response higher only 48.3% [45.9, 50.6] of the
   time; diff-means 73.1% [71.0, 75.1] — full per-encoder table in Appendix A.5, where the
   mpnet prototype falls to 40.9% [38.6, 43.2], below chance on pairs.
4. **The failure has an operating-point cost, not just a ranking cost.** At the
   validation-calibrated 5% false-safe target on MiniLM, safe acceptance is prototype 0.019 vs
   diff-means 0.245 on Aegis (12.9×), 0.061 vs 0.170 on SafeRLHF (2.8×), 0.082 vs 0.084 on
   BeaverTails (parity) (SS8).
5. **Prompt composition can fake a working prototype.** Full-population prompt-only two-centroid
   readouts reach 0.713 (BeaverTails) and 0.780 (SafeRLHF) with zero response information; on the
   prompt-matched primary cohorts the same readout collapses to 0.522 and exactly 0.500 (SS6).
6. **The finding replicates across architecture families and decoder scales.** Two SBERT
   encoders plus mean-pooled Qwen2.5-Instruct hidden states at 0.5B and 3B; the decoder geometry
   is the extreme case (class-mean cosines up to 0.9992) and Aegis inversion holds at both
   scales (SS9.2).
7. **The source control does real work.** On uncontrolled Aegis (`hash_all`), the source-prior
   shortcut alone scores 0.915 and response-length 0.684; on the jury-only control both collapse
   to 0.500/0.554 — without the control, the headline would be confounded (SS4.4, Appendix A).

### Core Decisions

- **"Similarity to safe" must declare its reference.** A prototype score inherits encoder
  origin, class concentration, and corpus composition. Response-safety readouts must name the
  negative or unlabeled reference distribution defining their direction.
- **One-class ≠ global.** Diff-means and RankOne are also global linear readouts and they work.
  The corrected axis is *unreferenced positive location* versus *referenced displacement*.
- **Prompt-disjoint is not prompt-controlled.** Response-level claims need a prompt-only
  diagnostic and, where possible, within-prompt or matched evaluation.
- **Below-chance AUC is diagnostic, not magic.** Flipping the score sign after seeing the test
  set is not a repair; identify the reference instead.

## When to Use This Report

### Scenario 1: "Can I score response safety as cosine-to-my-safe-centroid?"
No — not without a declared reference. SS4 shows the raw prototype is weak or inverted in every
controlled cell across four encoders; SS5 shows why (unidentified orientation); SS8 shows the
operating-point cost. Use a two-centroid/diff-means direction, a mixture-centered prototype, or
a supervised probe (SS10).

### Scenario 2: "My centroid-based safety/drift monitor shows a clean separation. Is it real?"
Run the SS6 diagnostic before believing it: evaluate the same readout on prompt-only inputs. If
prompt-only AUC is materially above chance, the separation is (at least partly) corpus
composition, not response behavior. Full-population BeaverTails/SafeRLHF prompt-only readouts
reach 0.713/0.780 with zero response information.

### Scenario 3: "Why does my one-class score prefer unsafe responses on some data?"
SS5: safe and unsafe class means are nearly collinear (cosines 0.825–0.999 on primary cohorts);
the sign of the expected gap is set by tiny norm/concentration differences, which are corpus
properties. On jury-labeled Aegis the unsafe mean is more concentrated, so the prototype inverts
with the whole CI below chance. Inverting the sign post hoc is not a fix (Core Decisions).

### Scenario 4: "What exactly does the FLMSec submission claim, and what may I not cite it for?"
The supported claim: positive-only centroid orientation is unidentified; controlled ranking is
weak or inverted; a declared reference improves ranking and held-out safe acceptance on two of
three corpora. Retired claims are enumerated in Claim Validation — do not cite the withdrawn
"at chance everywhere" / "all six transfers inverted" headlines from pre-v3 drafts.

## Metric Definitions

### Primary Metrics

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| ROC-AUC | Probability a randomly drawn safe response scores above a randomly drawn unsafe one; 0.5 = chance, < 0.5 = inverted |
| Safe prototype score | cos(z, μs/‖μs‖) — cosine of a response embedding to the normalized safe class mean |
| Two-centroid score | cos(z, μs) − cos(z, μu) |
| Diff-means score | projection onto (μs − μu)/‖μs − μu‖ |
| Centered-safe score | projection onto (μs − μm)/‖μs − μm‖, μm the unlabeled mixture mean — identical ranking to diff-means by the mixture identity μs − μm = πu(μs − μu) |
| RankOne | per-prompt direction aggregation following the concurrent RankOne protocol |
| Logistic probe | L2-regularized logistic regression on frozen embeddings — supervised reference point (not a "ceiling") |
| Expected gap Δ₊ | (‖μs‖² − μu·μs)/‖μs‖ — expected safe-minus-unsafe prototype score difference (Proposition 1 quantity) |
| Safe acceptance @ FS target | fraction of held-out safe responses accepted when the threshold is calibrated on validation to a false-safe (unsafe-accepted) rate target |
| Paired ranking accuracy | within mixed-label prompt (or native pair): fraction where the safe response outscores the unsafe one |

### Statistical Machinery

| Element | Contract |
|---|---|
| AUC uncertainty | 500 whole-prompt-cluster bootstrap resamples per cell; shared draws across methods for paired deltas; seed 20260715 |
| Operating-point uncertainty | 10,000 validation/test prompt-cluster draws; each draw recalibrates the threshold; train fit fixed |
| Threshold rule | accept iff score ≥ nextafter(descending unsafe validation score at floor(target × n_unsafe), +inf) — tie-conservative |
| Uncertainty scope | validation threshold + test sampling, **conditional on the fixed train fit** (no train-refit uncertainty claimed) |
| Splits | SHA-256 of salt + canonical prompt → 70/15/15 train/validation/test; prompt-disjoint by construction |
| Decontamination | exact-prompt overlap removal for transfer; cross-split response-overlap sensitivity for within-corpus (SS9.3) |

## SS1. Introduction

### SS1.1 Research Questions

1. Does cosine similarity to the mean embedding of known-safe responses identify response
   safety on real safety corpora, under controls that remove prompt-composition and
   label-source confounds?
2. If not, is the failure a property of the corpora, the encoders, or the rule itself — and can
   the same embeddings support a working readout with a declared reference?
3. What does the failure cost at a deployment-shaped operating point (fixed false-safe budget),
   rather than only in ranking metrics?

### SS1.2 Motivation

The construct is not hypothetical. Distance-to-reference-centroid scoring is a documented
industry pattern in embedding-drift monitoring (Evidently: drift scored as distance between mean
embeddings of current vs reference windows; Arize: Euclidean distance between the primary
dataset's centroid and the baseline's), and a 2025 sleeper-agent detector scores responses by
cosine distance to the mean safe-response embedding on the same MiniLM encoder audited here. The
drift construct declares both endpoints; the safety shortcut treats one class mean as if it were
a direction. That porting error is exactly the identification failure this study measures.
(References [4], [5], [6] respectively.)

This finding survived four adversarial passes, and its headline shrank at each one — the

## SS2. Methodology

### SS2.1 Design Principles

- **Remove the exploitable shortcuts before measuring.** Prompt-disjoint hash splits alone are
  insufficient (SS6); the primary cohorts additionally fix prompt composition (true mixed-label
  prompts), pair structure (native discordant pairs), or label source (jury-only).
- **Same embeddings, competing readouts.** Every readout — prototype, two-centroid, diff-means,
  centered-safe, RankOne, probe — consumes identical frozen embeddings, so differences are
  attributable to the readout, not the representation.
- **Controls that would catch our own confounds.** Response-length and source-prior shortcut
  baselines run in every cohort; geometry is measured, not assumed; overlap sensitivity is
  quantified (SS9.3).
- **Frozen provenance.** Dataset revisions, encoder revisions, ordered-row SHA-256 digests,
  split salt, bootstrap seed, package versions, and the exact command are recorded inside every
  result JSON; the release artifact carries a fail-closed verifier.

texts excluded), SHA-256 prompt-hash 70/15/15 splits on salt `one-class-centroid-v1-20260715`:

- **VRAM spill is a silent 25× slowdown, not an OOM.** The first Qwen-3B run at batch 32 filled
  11.9/12.3 GB and Windows CUDA spilled to shared memory: 3–5.5 s/batch, killed after 8 h at 47%
  of one array. Relaunched at batch 16: 8.5 GB resident, 1.5–2.0 batches/s, whole study in
  2 h 45 m. Check `nvidia-smi` headroom before long embed runs.
- **Co-tenants degrade silently.** A resident Ollama `llama-server` (~2.7 GB) mid-run pushed the
  rerun back into the spill regime until it idled out. Keep the GPU exclusive for long arms.
- **Caches are kill-safe at array granularity.** Embedding caches are keyed by ordered-row
  SHA-256 digest + encoder revision + corpus/split/mode and write only on array completion —
  a killed run resumes at the last completed array, not mid-array.

The release artifact is self-verifying: `verify_artifact.py` fail-closes on the archive
manifest, source hashes embedded in every result, frozen dataset/encoder revisions, ordered-row
digests, unique result cells, finite metrics, and the paper's expected primary AUCs. Suite: 33

In every cell where composition, pairing, or source cannot carry the signal, the unreferenced
prototype fails to deliver a usable ranking — while three referenced readouts on the same frozen
embeddings do. The failure is in the rule, not the representation.

## SS5. Measured Geometry Explains the Sign

### SS5.1 The Identification Result

Proposition 1: for unit-normalized embeddings, the expected prototype gap is
Δ₊ = (‖μs‖² − μu·μs)/‖μs‖, which has no fixed sign — it is positive iff ‖μs‖ > ‖μu‖·cos(μs, μu).
Nothing about "being the safe mean" pins the sign; class concentration does. The mixture
identity μs − μm = πu(μs − μu) further implies the *centered* prototype is collinear with the
supervised difference direction — verified empirically: centered-safe equals diff-means in
ranking in every cell (Appendix A). **Falsifier:** a cell with decisively nonzero Δ₊ whose AUC
lands on the opposite side of chance would break the identity's predictive claim; none occurs
across 12 cells (SS5.3), and both sign misses sit where the identity predicts ≈ chance.

### SS5.2b Two Geometric Regimes, One Failure

The table splits into regimes. **SBERT regime:** short class means (norms 0.19–0.33) with
moderate collinearity (0.83–0.97) — the gap has room to move, and where it is decisively
negative (all Aegis, SafeRLHF mpnet) the inversion is strong. **Decoder regime:** near-unit
class means (0.91–0.96) at extreme collinearity (≥ 0.9969 on primary cohorts) — the gap is
compressed toward zero, so paired-corpus cells hover at chance while the concentrated Aegis
unsafe mean still forces the sign negative at both scales. Same identity, two presentations:
the SBERT cells show the *sign* pathology most clearly; the decoder cells show the
*identification* pathology most clearly (a third-decimal norm difference is all that decides
which class "wins").

### SS5.3 Sign Agreement Is 10/12 — and the Two Misses Are Informative

Both disagreements (mpnet/BeaverTails, Qwen-3B/SafeRLHF) occur where |Δ₊| ≤ 0.005 — the gap is
within noise of zero and the observed AUC is within noise of chance. The mean-gap identity
predicts an *expected score difference*, not AUC; where it is decisively nonzero (Aegis: −0.006
to −0.035), it calls the inversion correctly on all four encoders. Where it is ≈ 0, the cell is
at chance. There is no cell where a decisively positive gap coexists with an inverted AUC. The
association is quantified in SS10.5 (Pearson r = 0.855, Spearman ρ = 0.881 across the 12 cells;
binomial p = 0.019 for ≥ 10/12 sign matches under a coin-flip null).

### SS5.4 The Decoder Arms Are the Extreme Case

Mean-pooled decoder embeddings concentrate mass in a dominant direction: class-mean norms rise
to 0.91–0.96, with primary-cohort cosines ≥ 0.9969 (diagnostic cohorts span 0.9678–0.9992;
Appendix B) versus 0.19–0.33 norms and 0.83–0.97 primary cosines for SBERT. The
prototype's fate is then decided by third-decimal norm differences — and the Aegis unsafe mean
is again more concentrated, so the inversion survives the architecture change at both scales.

### SS5.5 The Confounds Are Visible in the Geometry — and There Are Two of Them

On Aegis `mixed_prompt_fit_eval` (source-confounded, non-primary), cos(μs, μu) collapses to
0.51/0.45 (MiniLM/mpnet) with Δ₊ ≈ +0.28 — the class means separate by *source*, not safety,
and the prototype scores 0.884–0.951 there (Appendix A). The same cohorts carry a second
saturated shortcut: train-fitted response log-length alone scores **0.991** there (Appendix A,
LEN column) versus 0.554 on the jury control — source identity and response length are jointly
confounded with the label outside the controlled arm. Geometry and shortcut baselines together
flag what the AUC alone would hide.

### SS5.6 Conclusion

The failure mechanism is measured, not conjectured: near-collinear class means + small,
corpus-determined norm differences ⇒ unidentified sign. The geometry also predicts where the
prototype *appears* to work — exactly where a confound has separated the means.

until a prompt-only ablation says otherwise.

evaluate on another corpus's hash-test split after exact-prompt decontamination

control here, not a novelty claim.

## SS8. Validation-Calibrated Operating Points

### SS8.1 Hypothesis and Why This Section Exists

Ranking metrics understate deployment cost. The v3 protocol calibrates an acceptance threshold
on validation to a false-safe budget (5% or 10%), then measures held-out safe acceptance —
"how much safe traffic can this readout actually pass at a fixed leak rate?" This closed an
archival gate (operating-point analysis) flagged in earlier reviews. Hypothesis: if the
ranking failure is operationally irrelevant (e.g., both readouts unusable at deployment
budgets), prototype and diff-means acceptance should be comparable everywhere — which would
weaken the practical stakes of the identification result. BeaverTails behaves exactly that way
(parity); SafeRLHF and Aegis do not.

## SS10. Cross-Section Synthesis

### SS10.1 One Mechanism, Five Symptoms

A single geometric fact — near-collinear class means with corpus-determined concentration
differences — produces: (1) at-chance paired-cohort ranking, (2) the Aegis inversion, (3) the
transfer inversions specifically into Aegis, (4) the decoder arms' amplified version of both,
and (5) the apparent "success" on source-confounded cohorts where a confound, not safety,
separates the means. No additional hypothesis is needed for any of the five.

Quantified across the 12 primary cells (all four geometry quantities from Appendix B against
both AUC outcomes; `analysis/secondary_stats_v1.json`):

| Predictor | vs prototype AUC (r / ρ) | vs diff-means AUC (r / ρ) |
|---|---|---|
| Expected gap Δ₊ | **+0.855 / +0.881** | −0.645 / −0.734 |
| ‖μs‖ − ‖μu‖ | +0.731 / +0.671 | −0.550 / −0.420 |
| cos(μs, μu) | +0.548 / +0.378 | −0.386 / −0.189 |
| ‖μs − μu‖ | −0.691 / −0.615 | +0.524 / +0.413 |

The full identity Δ₊ dominates its own ingredients as a predictor of the prototype's fate —
and correlates *negatively* with diff-means AUC: the corpora where the prototype inverts
hardest are the ones where the referenced direction has the most signal to recover.

### SS10.2 Why the Referenced Readouts Work

Diff-means, centered-safe, two-centroid, and RankOne all subtract a second location before
projecting — they measure *displacement*, which the mixture identity ties to the true class
contrast even when the reference is unlabeled. The probe learns the same direction with a bias
term. The prototype alone measures *location relative to the origin*, and the origin is an
artifact of the encoder, not of safety.

### SS10.3 Relation to RankOne (division of labor)

RankOne (concurrent, verified real on OpenReview) establishes what works: a single direction
suffices for response-level safety classification. This study establishes what fails to be
identified — and shows measured class-mean geometry predicts the failure. The transfer section
corroborates RankOne's positive results; the identification result and the confound diagnostics
are the non-overlapping contribution.

### SS10.4 What This Buys the Chimera Stack

TDD002's constitutional router historically used centroid-similarity readouts; P123.0 fixed a
real centroid-persistence bug and P124 shipped the supervised-direction routing upgrade. This
study is the controlled, publishable justification for that engineering direction: unreferenced
prototype scores are not a valid routing signal; referenced directions on the same embeddings
are — and cheaply.

### SS10.6 What This Rules Out — and What It Does Not

**Ruled out** (by the displayed evidence): the raw safe prototype as a response-safety ranking
rule on these corpora/encoders (H0); data starvation as the cause (H2); SBERT-specificity
(H3); train/test-alignment luck (SS7); resample-count and salt artifacts (C2/C4); cohort
selection as the driver (C1); response-duplicate leakage as the driver (C3).

**Not ruled out** (and not claimed): that *some* one-class method works (support estimators,
Deep SVDD, PU learning are untested — only the raw centroid is); that the geometry holds for
decoders beyond 3B or outside the Qwen family; that a safety-specialized or
instruction-contrastive encoder escapes the collinearity regime; adversarial or multilingual
robustness of the referenced readouts; and any operating point beyond MiniLM's. These are
exactly the archival gates (SS14.2).

## SS13. Conclusions

The mean embedding of safe responses is a location, not an identified safety direction. Across
three public response-safety corpora, four frozen encoders spanning two architecture families
and two decoder scales, controlled prompt/pair/source cohorts, two extra split salts, and six
decontaminated transfers, cosine similarity to the unreferenced safe mean never delivers a
usable ranking and is significantly inverted wherever class concentration differs — exactly as
the measured geometry predicts. Referenced directions recover useful rankings and materially
higher safe acceptance at fixed false-safe budgets from the same embeddings. For
response-safety measurement: declare the reference, audit prompt-only predictability, and treat
below-chance AUC as a symptom of an unidentified readout, not a sign to flip.

## SS14. Limitations and Follow-Up

### SS14.1 Limitations

- Two English SBERT-family encoders + one decoder family (Qwen2.5-Instruct) at two scales; no
  safety-specialized encoders, no multilingual, no adversarial obfuscation.
- Aegis is jury-labeled and source-controlled — an auxiliary control, not a human-labeled
  paired replication; the pronounced within-corpus inversion is confined to that arm (SafeRLHF
  adds two milder fully-below-chance cells; SS9.5).
- Bootstrap intervals are conditional on the fixed train fit (no train-refit uncertainty).
- Tests a raw positive centroid, not the full one-class family (OC-SVM, Deep SVDD, PU
  learning) — the paper's scope statement is explicit about this.
- Operating-point analysis is MiniLM-only (the paper's Table 3 contract).
- **Not pre-registered, and partially post hoc:** the v2→v3 protocol additions and claim
  retirements followed an audit that had seen v2 results; the salt, decoder, and 3B arms were
  review-driven additions. Mitigations: salts, seeds, and cohort rules are frozen in the
  released harness; every revision made the claims *narrower*; and the C1–C4 battery (SS9.4)
  checks the analyst-controlled axes explicitly. The report-level statistics in SS10.5 were
  likewise computed after the cells were known and are labeled as descriptive of this fixed
  evidence set, not as confirmatory tests of a pre-stated hypothesis.

### SS14.2 Follow-Up Gates (archival version)

1. Second decoder family (not scale) — e.g., a Llama- or Gemma-family arm.
2. Category-level analysis (which harm categories drive the inversion).
3. An independent human-labeled non-paired corpus (removes the jury-label caveat).
4. Operating points across all four encoders.

## Appendix M: Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Safe prototype / one-class centroid | Normalized mean of safe-response embeddings, used directly as a scoring direction |
| Reference distribution | The second location (unsafe mean, or unlabeled mixture mean) that turns a location into a direction |
| Identification failure | The score's sign/orientation is not determined by the construct it claims to measure |
| Prompt-matched cohort | Evaluation restricted so prompt composition cannot predict the label |
| Source control | Cohort restricted to a single label source so source identity cannot predict the label |
| Discordant native pairs | Same-prompt response pairs with opposite safety labels, as released by the corpus |
| Δ₊ (expected gap) | Expected safe-minus-unsafe prototype score; Proposition 1 quantity |
| False-safe rate | Fraction of unsafe responses accepted as safe at a threshold |
| Prompt-cluster bootstrap | Resampling whole prompts (with all their responses) to respect within-prompt correlation |
| Frozen-byte release | Artifact set attested by hash; any edit requires re-attestation |
| Schema v3 | Result-JSON generation adding overlap decontamination + operating points; the citable evidence base |
